A building made of other buildings

Sixty years of Sea Shanty, two years of nothing, and a rebuild put together almost entirely from salvage.

Ein stori

Since 2016, in the same spot as always

The original Sea Shanty traded for sixty-odd years before it closed in 2002. Nothing quite filled the hole it left in Trearddur Bay — the village needed an amenity in the same place, but in a modern form, and something that gave the bay a proper sense of place.

The building standing here now opened in July 2016: larger premises, a licence, a bigger kitchen, more seating and a much longer menu. The interior was built by craftsmen from the island, and almost everything in it is recycled.

A memorial to beaches and boating, with an Edwardian ice cream parlour and a nickelodeon that still works.

Some of what's on the walls tells the history of Trearddur Bay, Holyhead and Anglesey. The rest is there purely to make people stop and look — children especially, which was part of the brief from the start.

In summer we employ close to eighty people from around the island, and about sixty the rest of the year. That scale is also what lets us put a meaningful amount back into local causes, which matters a great deal to the owners.

Beth rydyn ni'n ei wneud

Four things, done properly

The building does a lot at once — restaurant, bar, coffee shop and ice cream parlour.
Here's what each of them actually is.

Amazing food

Fresh fish landed a few miles up the coast, a specials board that changes daily, and a standing menu that runs from small plates to a proper steak sandwich. Gluten free options right across it.

See the menu

Fully stocked bar

Up to three rotating cask ales from Conwy Brewery and Snowdon Craft Brewery, plus San Miguel, Guinness and Heineken on draught. A short wine list, kept short so every bottle earns its place — some by the glass, some served in our own jugs.

Coffee & cakes

Small-batch coffee roasted by Chartley, a second-generation family business we've worked with since the 1990s. Cakes from Clams, a relationship going back almost twenty years. Served all day.

Ice cream parlour

An Edwardian counter with a fully restored nickelodeon beside it, open from ten in the morning until ten at night. Cones, tubs and sundaes built in front of you, with a takeaway hatch round the side when the restaurant's full.

Cyflenwyr lleol

Who we buy from

Most of these relationships predate the current building by a decade or more.

Chartley Coffee

A family roaster in its second generation, working with small and large customers across the UK. Neil, one of our directors, found them in the 1990s while working for a then-small pub company. Ethically produced, small-batch, and the only coffee we've ever wanted to serve.

Clams Cakes

Good coffee needs good cake. We've bought from Clams for the best part of twenty years and they still make everything by hand.

Conwy Brewery

One of the two North Wales breweries whose cask ales rotate through our pumps.

Snowdon Craft Brewery

The other. Between them they keep up to three changing local ales on at any time.
